Household water-safety plan builder

Storage targets, purification method on hand, ORS packets on hand; links to a future PlainPrep go-bag without duplicating it.

What a water safety plan actually is

The WHO frames a Water Safety Plan around three steps: assess where risk could enter your water (source, storage, handling), put simple controls in place for each risk you find, and check in on it periodically rather than treating it as a one-time task. This builder applies the same three steps to a single household instead of a utility.

Source: WHO — Water safety planning

What your plan should cover

Three things, kept in sync with the rest of PlainWater: how much stored water you're targeting and how you're rotating it (see the water-needs calculator and storage guides in Sourcing & Storage), which purification method you actually have on hand and know how to use (see Purification Methods), and whether you have oral rehydration salts on hand in case of illness (see the ORS calculator in Illness Recognition). A plan that names all three, specifically, is far more useful in an actual emergency than a general intention to "be prepared."

Source: WHO — Water safety planning

Revisit it, don't just write it once

WHO's framework treats monitoring as one of the three core pillars, not an afterthought — a plan that's never rechecked drifts out of date as stored water ages, household size changes, or your travel/disaster risk changes with the season.

Source: WHO — Water safety planning
