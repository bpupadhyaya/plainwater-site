1 Educational Guidance, Not Diagnosis or Certification

PlainWater provides general educational information about water safety, water purification, and basic waterborne-illness recognition. Nothing in PlainWater — including this website's module browser — is medical advice, a medical device, or a substitute for a certified laboratory water test. No feature diagnoses a medical condition or certifies that any specific water sample is safe to drink.

2 "When In Doubt, Treat or Boil It"

Appearance, smell, and taste are unreliable indicators of water safety — many of the most dangerous contaminants (including several disease-causing pathogens and chemicals such as arsenic) have no color, odor, or taste at all. PlainWater's guidance always errs toward treating or boiling water before drinking it when safety is uncertain, rather than assuming water is safe because it looks or smells fine.

3 Visual and Camera-Based Features Are Reference Only

Any visual, odor, or camera-based comparison feature in PlainWater is strictly educational and reference-only. No on-device sensor or camera feature can reliably detect pathogens or certify water safety, and PlainWater never makes that claim.

4 Medical Emergencies

If you believe you or someone else may be experiencing a medical emergency — including severe dehydration, signs of a serious waterborne illness, or any other urgent medical condition — contact your local emergency services immediately. Do not rely on PlainWater or this website in place of professional emergency medical care.

5 Citations Are Informational

PlainWater's guidance is cited to free, public, authoritative sources (WHO, CDC, EPA, USGS, Ready.gov/FEMA, the American Red Cross, UNICEF/WHO's Joint Monitoring Programme, and CAWST). Citations are provided so you can read the underlying source yourself; they do not constitute a guarantee that the cited guidance applies to your specific situation, location, or water source. Regulations, guidelines, and best practices can change — always check the cited source directly for the most current version, and consult a qualified professional or your local health authority when in doubt.
